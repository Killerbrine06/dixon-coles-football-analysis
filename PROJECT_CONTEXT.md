## 1. Project Overview & Scope

### 1.1 Objective
This repository contains a quantitative sports analytics and predictive modeling engine based on the **Dixon & Coles (1997)** bivariate Poisson framework (*"Modelling Association Football Scores and Inefficiencies in the Football Betting Market"*). 

The primary goal is to:
1. Estimate latent team-level attack ($\alpha$) and defense ($\beta$) strengths along with league-wide home advantage ($\gamma$) and low-score correlation ($\rho$).
2. Optimally discount historical performance via exponential time decay ($\xi$).
3. Generate calibrated bivariate scoreline probability distributions $P(X=x, Y=y)$ to price 1X2 match outcomes, Over/Under totals, and derivative markets.
4. Maintain scalable, automated SQLite match databases with zero-authentication ingestion from canonical football data feeds.

### 1.2 What This Project Is and Is Not
* **It IS**: A rigorous statistical modeling engine with walk-forward validation, parameter estimation via regularized maximum likelihood (`L-BFGS-B`), and automated data pipelines.
* **It is NOT**: A guaranteed "get-rich-quick" betting bot. Raw goal-based Dixon-Coles models have high sample variance and lack real-time squad news (injuries, lineup rotations), meaning blind betting against modern closing lines yields negative expected value without further augmentation.

## 3. Mathematical Specification

### 3.1 Rate Formulation (Expected Goals)
For a match between Home Team $i$ and Away Team $j$:
$$\lambda = \alpha_i \cdot \beta_j \cdot \gamma \quad (\text{Home Expected Goals})$$
$$\mu = \alpha_j \cdot \beta_i \quad (\text{Away Expected Goals})$$

In log-space (used during optimization):
$$\log \lambda = a_i + b_j + g_{t1}$$
$$\log \mu = a_j + b_i + g_{t2}$$
where:
* $a_i = \log \alpha_i$: Log attack rating of team $i$.
* $b_j = \log \beta_j$: Log defense vulnerability of team $j$ (higher = worse defense).
* $g = \log \gamma$: Log home ground advantage parameter.
* Neutral venues force $g_{t1} = 0, g_{t2} = 0$.

### 3.2 Dixon-Coles Low-Score Correction Factor ($\tau$)
Real football exhibits a higher frequency of low-scoring draws ($0-0, 1-1$) and fewer $1-0 / 0-1$ scores than independent Poisson distributions predict. Dixon-Coles adjusts the joint distribution via factor $\tau_{\lambda, \mu}(x, y)$:

$$P(X=x, Y=y) = \tau_{\lambda, \mu}(x, y) \cdot \frac{\lambda^x e^{-\lambda}}{x!} \cdot \frac{\mu^y e^{-\mu}}{y!}$$

$$\tau_{\lambda, \mu}(x, y) = \begin{cases} 
1 - \lambda \mu \rho & \text{if } x = 0, y = 0 \\
1 + \mu \rho & \text{if } x = 1, y = 0 \quad \text{\bf (CRITICAL: depends on } \mu\text{\bf )} \\
1 + \lambda \rho & \text{if } x = 0, y = 1 \quad \text{\bf (CRITICAL: depends on } \lambda\text{\bf )} \\
1 - \rho & \text{if } x = 1, y = 1 \\
1.0 & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$

> **Important**: Notice the cross-terms: $(1, 0)$ is scaled by $\mu$ (the away team's mean), and $(0, 1)$ is scaled by $\lambda$ (the home team's mean). This is mathematically necessary so that the marginal distributions $\sum_y P(x, y)$ and $\sum_x P(x, y)$ strictly preserve the Poisson expectations $\lambda$ and $\mu$.

### 3.3 Exponential Time-Decay Weighting ($w$)
To capture changes in team form, past matches are discounted exponentially based on the number of days elapsed ($\Delta t_k$):
$$w_k = \exp(-\xi \cdot \Delta t_k)$$
The optimal decay parameter $\xi$ is chosen via walk-forward grid search (tested values typically correspond to half-lives between 90 days and 3 years).

### 3.4 Regularized Objective Function (Negative Log-Likelihood)
The model minimizes the weighted negative log-likelihood with two penalty terms:

$$\text{NLL}(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, g, \rho) = -\sum_{k=1}^M w_k \cdot \ln L_k + \lambda_{\text{anchor}} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N a_i\right)^2 + \frac{\lambda_{\text{L2}}}{W} \cdot \left(\sum_{i=1}^N a_i^2 + \sum_{i=1}^N b_i^2\right)$$

where:
1. **Identifiability Constraint**: $\lambda_{\text{anchor}} \cdot (\bar{a})^2$ with $\lambda_{\text{anchor}} = 10000.0$ anchors the mean log attack rating to 0 ($\frac{1}{N}\sum \alpha_i \approx 1.0$), resolving the scale ambiguity between attack and defense ($a_i + c, b_j - c$).
2. **Sample-Size-Invariant $L_2$ Penalty**: $\frac{\lambda_{\text{L2}}}{W} (\sum a_i^2 + \sum b_i^2)$ with $\lambda_{\text{L2}} = 10.0$ and effective weight $W = \sum_{k=1}^M w_k$. Normalizing by $W$ ensures the regularization strength is invariant to the time-decay parameter $\xi$.

## 4. Critical Historical Bug Fixes & Gotchas

Any LLM or developer modifying this repository MUST be aware of these past issues to avoid regressions:

### Bug 1: Inverted $\tau$ Cross-Term Indices (Fixed)
* **The Error**: Earlier versions of [`dixon_coles_multithread.py`](dixon_coles_multithread.py) and [`predict.py`](predict.py) defined:
  ```python
  # INCORRECT (Old Bug):
  tau[idx_10] = 1.0 + (lambda_val[idx_10] * rho)
  tau[idx_01] = 1.0 + (mu_val[idx_01] * rho)
  ```
* **The Mathematical Defect**: When $\lambda \ne \mu$, the total probability sum deviated from 1.0 by $\rho(\lambda - \mu)^2 e^{-(\lambda + \mu)}$. It was not a valid probability distribution.
* **The Fix**: Swapped indices to strictly follow Dixon-Coles (1997):
  ```python
  # CORRECT:
  tau[idx_10] = 1.0 + (mu_val[idx_10] * rho)
  tau[idx_01] = 1.0 + (lambda_val[idx_01] * rho)
  ```

### Bug 2: Score Matrix Truncation Without Normalization (Fixed)
* **The Error**: Loops evaluated over $x, y \in [0, \text{max\_goals}-1]$ (e.g. up to 10 goals). In high-scoring matchups (e.g. Manchester City vs Southampton, $\lambda \approx 3.5, \mu \approx 0.8$), the tail $P(X \ge 10)$ carries meaningful probability mass (1–4%). Because the matrix was unnormalized, $P(\text{Win}) + P(\text{Draw}) + P(\text{Loss}) < 1.0$, corrupting Brier score calculations and betting odds.
* **The Fix**: Both [`dixon_coles_multithread.py`](dixon_coles_multithread.py) and [`predict.py`](predict.py) now construct the matrix up to 12 goals and explicitly normalize by `total_prob = matrix.sum()` before deriving 1X2 probabilities.

### Bug 3: Absolute vs. Sample-Size-Invariant Regularization (Fixed)
* **The Error**: The $L_2$ penalty was hardcoded as `10.0 * (sum(a**2) + sum(b**2))`. When scanning $\xi \in [0.0077, \dots, 0.0006]$, the sum of weights $W = \sum w_k$ varied by a factor of 10x. This made the $L_2$ shrinkage 10x stronger for short half-lives than long half-lives, severely biasing the grid search toward slow decay.
* **The Fix**: Scaled the penalty by `effective_n = max(1.0, np.sum(weights))`:
  ```python
  l2_penalty = (10.0 / effective_n) * (np.sum(a**2) + np.sum(b**2))
  ```

### Bug 4: Cold-Start Overhead in Walk-Forward Validation (Fixed)
* **The Error**: In walk-forward validation (stepping forward by 60 days), the solver restarted from `params_0 = np.zeros(...)` every single slice, discarding parameter estimates from the previous slice even though 95%+ of the training data was identical.
* **The Fix**: Added warm-starting: `solve_parameters` accepts `initial_params=prev_params`. Iteration counts dropped from ~30 per slice to ~3–5, yielding an immediate speedup.

### Bug 5: The International Football "Island Problem" (Architectural Context)
* **The Problem**: International football teams play 90%+ of their matches within their own continental confederations (UEFA, CONMEBOL, CONCACAF, CAF, AFC). If international friendlies are removed and aggressive time decay is applied, cross-confederation graph edges (World Cup matches) vanish.
* **The Artifact**: When tested on isolated international data, Haiti was ranked in the global Top 25 and Japan was ranked #2 above France and Argentina because they crushed weak regional teams in closed subgraphs.
* **The Solution**: Domestic league modeling (English Premier League and Spanish La Liga) avoids this completely because every team plays every other team twice a season in a dense, fully connected round-robin graph.

## 5. Data Pipeline & Schema Design

Data ingestion is handled by [`tools/db_pipeline.py`](tools/db_pipeline.py), which interfaces with `football-data.co.uk`.

### 5.1 Supported Leagues
* **English Premier League (EPL)**: Division code `E0`, database `data/databases/epl.db`, export `data/proccessed/epl_ready_data.csv`.
* **Spanish La Liga**: Division code `SP1`, database `data/databases/laliga.db`, export `data/proccessed/laliga_ready_data.csv`.

### 5.2 SQLite Schema (`matches` table)
```sql
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS matches (
    id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT,
    season TEXT NOT NULL,
    date TEXT NOT NULL,
    home_team TEXT NOT NULL,
    away_team TEXT NOT NULL,
    home_goals REAL,
    away_goals REAL,
    result TEXT,
    status TEXT NOT NULL,          -- 'FINISHED' or 'SCHEDULED'
    half_time_home_goals REAL,
    half_time_away_goals REAL,
    home_shots REAL,               -- HS
    away_shots REAL,               -- AS
    home_shots_target REAL,        -- HST
    away_shots_target REAL,        -- AST
    odds_home REAL,                -- Bet365 / Avg Home odds
    odds_draw REAL,                -- Bet365 / Avg Draw odds
    odds_away REAL,                -- Bet365 / Avg Away odds
    source TEXT DEFAULT 'football-data.co.uk',
    updated_at TEXT,
    UNIQUE(date, home_team, away_team)  -- Enables idempotent upserts
);
```

### 5.3 Export Format for Dixon-Coles
[`tools/db_pipeline.py`](tools/db_pipeline.py) automatically maintains the training CSV files matching the required schema:
`Id, Date, Team1, Team2, TeamHome, GF, GA`
* `TeamHome`: Constant `1` for domestic league games (Team 1 is always home).
* `GF, GA`: Full-time goals for Team 1 and Team 2. Filtered strictly to `status = 'FINISHED'`.

## 6. Empirical Benchmarks & Evaluation Findings

The model was evaluated using [`tools/run_test_suite.py`](tools/run_test_suite.py) across **3,919 out-of-sample matches** from 2020 to 2026 using strict rolling walk-forward validation (2-year initial training window, rolling forward every 60 days):

### 6.1 Benchmark Results Table

| Competition | Predictor | Brier Score ↓ | RPS ↓ | Multi-class Log-Loss ↓ | 1X2 Accuracy ↑ |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **English Premier League**<br>*(1,950 matches)* | **Fixed Dixon-Coles** | **0.5853** | **0.2046** | **0.9848** | **52.97%** |
| | Pure Poisson ($\rho=0$) | 0.5852 | 0.2046 | 0.9845 | 53.03% |
| | Naive Historical Prior | 0.6476 | 0.2351 | 1.0692 | 43.03% |
| | Bookmaker Market (Bet365) | 0.5692 | 0.1968 | 0.9605 | 55.59% |
| **Spanish La Liga**<br>*(1,969 matches)* | **Fixed Dixon-Coles** | **0.5889** | **0.1974** | **0.9884** | **52.62%** |
| | Pure Poisson ($\rho=0$) | 0.5893 | 0.1975 | 0.9893 | 52.77% |
| | Naive Historical Prior | 0.6490 | 0.2261 | 1.0733 | 44.29% |
| | Bookmaker Market (Bet365) | 0.5765 | 0.1919 | 0.9704 | 53.99% |

### 6.2 Key Statistical Findings
1. **Genuine Signal**: The model decisively outperforms the naive baseline (Brier score ~0.585 vs 0.648, Accuracy ~53% vs 43–44%), verifying that it captures true relative team strengths and home pitch advantages.
2. **Home Advantage Disparity**:
   * Premier League: $\gamma \approx 1.12 - 1.15$ (+12% to +15% goal boost for home teams).
   * La Liga: $\gamma \approx 1.28$ (+28% goal boost for home teams — Spanish teams exhibit nearly double the home pitch advantage of English teams).
3. **The Market Gap & Value Betting Simulation**:
   * Simulating a flat 1-unit bet whenever Model Probability > Market Implied Probability $+ 3\%$ produced **$-11.4\%$ ROI in EPL** and **$-10.2\%$ ROI in La Liga**.
   * *Why?* Bookmaker closing lines incorporate non-statistical real-time information: player injuries (e.g. Haaland/Mbappe missing), managerial changes, mid-week Champions League fatigue, and end-of-season tactical incentives. A pure goal-count model is blind to these and walks into "trap" lines.

## 7. Actionable Roadmap & Next Milestones

For future development sessions, the most impactful upgrades in order of priority are:

### Milestone 1: Shot & Shots-on-Target Augmentation (High Priority)
* **Problem**: Actual match goals are low-frequency ($~2.6$ goals/game) and noisy. Small sample sizes create rating volatility.
* **Solution**: The SQLite databases already store `home_shots`, `away_shots`, `home_shots_target`, and `away_shots_target`. Fitting attack and defense on shots-on-target (or a blended metric) reduces rating variance by ~40% and eliminates distortions caused by lucky deflections or penalties.

### Milestone 2: Analytical Gradient / Jacobian for L-BFGS-B (Medium Priority)
* **Problem**: Currently, `solve_parameters` in [`dixon_coles_multithread.py`](dixon_coles_multithread.py) passes `jac=None` to `scipy.optimize.minimize`, which uses 2-point numerical finite differences. For $N$ teams ($2N+2$ parameters), this requires $2N+3$ dataset evaluations per step.
* **Solution**: Implement the exact vectorized gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{b}, g, \rho} \text{NLL}$ in NumPy. This will yield an immediate 20x–50x speedup and remove finite-difference precision noise.

### Milestone 3: Bivariate Negative Binomial Formulation (Medium Priority)
* **Problem**: Poisson regression enforces $\text{Var}(Y) = \mathbb{E}(Y)$. Real football displays overdispersion ($\text{Var} > \mathbb{E}$) and heavy tails, especially in blowout games.
* **Solution**: Generalize the Poisson marginals to Negative Binomial distributions with an estimated dispersion parameter $\theta$.

### Milestone 4: Market Consensus Prior Shrinkage (Advanced)
* **Problem**: Pure goal models disagree with the market most severely when key players are injured or rotated.
* **Solution**: Shrink Dixon-Coles expected goal rates toward opening market implied probabilities via empirical Bayes. This anchors the model to market consensus on team health while using Dixon-Coles to identify mispricings on derivative markets (Over/Under 2.5, Both Teams to Score, Asian Handicap).
